# Multi-item timing models — research for issue #50 (Build a Sentence)

Researched 2026-08-22 from primary sources only (ets.org, pearsonpte.com, englishtest.duolingo.com, collegeboard.org, cambridgeenglish.org, ielts.org/ielts.idp.com, gmac.com, moodle source + docs, 1EdTech QTI). Informs issue #50, "Time Build a Sentence as one budget, not 40 seconds per Item".

## What ETS publishes (and does not)

- **The issue's claim about the Blueprint is CONFIRMED, and its figures are exact.** The [2026 Test Blueprint and Specifications](https://www.eu.ets.org/pdfs/toefl/toefl-ibt-test-specifications-2026.pdf) Blueprint Table (p. 1) gives Writing as **12 items / 20 raw points / "Estimated Time 23 min"**. The Writing Section table (p. 5) repeats **"12 / 23 min"** as a single cell spanning all three task types; Build a Sentence's own row carries **items (10), CEFR (A1–C2), Machine Scored (10) — and no time cell at all**.
- **The word "second" does not appear anywhere in the Blueprint.** Every time it publishes is a section-level estimate: Reading 18–21 min + 9 min router, Listening 18 min + 7/11 min module, Writing 23 min, Speaking 8 min, Min/Max test time 1 hr 23 min / 1 hr 29 min. No per-item or per-task figure for any of the 120 items.
- **But the Blueprint is not the only ETS document, and the issue's wording overreaches there.** The companion [Updated TOEFL iBT Test Overview](https://www.ets.org/pdfs/toefl/toefl-ibt-test-overview.pdf) **does publish per-task timing for three of the five task types** — and pointedly not for Build a Sentence:
  - Write an Email (p. 14): 7 minutes for the task.
  - Write for an Academic Discussion (p. 15): 10 minutes for the task.
  - Listen and Repeat (p. 17): at most 8 to 12 seconds to record the response to each sentence.
  - Take an Interview (p. 19): 45 seconds to answer each question.
  - Build a Sentence (p. 13): a full task description and worked example with **no timing sentence of any kind**.
- **So the accurate claim is narrower and stronger: ETS times every 2026 task it chooses to time — except this one.** The 40 s in `shared/src/schemas.ts:653` remains this project's invention; but the *reason* it is unpublished is not "ETS publishes no per-item timing", it is that Build a Sentence is the one task ETS left out of an otherwise complete per-task timing list.
- **The residual is therefore arithmetic ETS itself supports.** Overview p. 4 gives Writing 12 items and an approximate base time of 23 minutes. 23 min − 7 min (email) − 10 min (discussion) = **6 min = 360 s covering all ten Build a Sentence items plus that part's directions**. The two published figures are already the literals this repo encodes (`timeLimitSec: z.literal(420)`, `z.literal(600)` at `shared/src/schemas.ts:699,706`), so the subtraction is internally consistent with the codebase's own fidelity anchors.
- **TOEFL Practice Online times by section, not by item.** [TPO FAQ](https://www.ets.org/s/toefl/pdf/toefl_tpo_faq.pdf): timed mode applies the real test's limit to each of the four sections, and untimed mode drops the section limits. Section is the granularity ETS's own practice product exposes.
- **The retired TOEFL Essentials is the closest ETS analogue, and it answers the pooled-vs-per-item question directly.** The [ETS Essentials Writing content page](https://www.ets.org/toefl/test-takers/essentials/content/writing.html) publishes **14–18 Build a Sentence questions plus two writing tasks** in a section of **24–30 minutes** — again a section total with no per-item figure.
- **ETS's own Essentials practice-test client publishes the section directions, and they are decisive.** In [TOEFL Essentials Practice Test 1](https://www.ets.org/s/toefl-essentials/free-practice-test-1/start1.html) (directions text embedded in the delivery client on ets.org), the Writing section directions say the section is **split into parts, each with its own timer**, and that in the first part the test taker moves words and phrases into blanks to build grammatical sentences. A second variant in the same file counts three separately timed parts.
- **The same client publishes back-navigation for the Build a Sentence part explicitly.** The task directions say that within this part of the Writing section Next moves on, **a skipped question can be revisited later for as long as the part's time lasts**, and Back returns to earlier questions.
- **ETS's granularity rule is visible across sections in that same file: clock granularity and navigation travel together.**
  - Essentials **Listening** (per-question clock): each question has a short time limit, shown on a per-question clock, and once Next is selected the previous question cannot be reopened.
  - Essentials **Reading** (section clock): the clock counts down the whole section, questions can be skipped and revisited with Back, and a review screen is available.
  - Essentials **Speaking** (per-response clock): when a response's time runs out the test moves to the next task, with no way back.
  - Essentials **writing essays** (per-task clock): the clock counts down each question or task, and **when it runs out the test moves to the next question**.
- **No primary source found** for a 2026 iBT unauthenticated practice client exposing Build a Sentence timing. The 2026 sample tests ([sample-test-jan-2026-1](https://www.ets.org/toefl/test-takers/ibt/prepare/sample-test-jan-2026-1.html)) are behind a lead-capture form; the open [free-practice writing client](https://www.ets.org/s/toefl/free-practice/writing.html) is still the pre-2026 iBT (Integrated 20 min, Independent 30 min) and has no Build a Sentence at all. The [2026 upcoming-updates page](https://www.ets.org/toefl/test-takers/ibt/upcoming-updates-jan-2026.html) and the [2026 Writing content page](https://www.ets.org/toefl/test-takers/ibt/about/content/writing.html) publish no timings and no navigation rules.

## How other tests time multi-item tasks

### Pearson PTE Academic

- **Per-item timing is exhaustive in Speaking & Writing and absent in Reading** — the split is the interesting part. [Test format: Speaking & Writing](https://www.pearsonpte.com/pte-academic/test-format/speaking-writing/) publishes a discrete prep/record budget for every item type: Read Aloud 30–40 s prep; Repeat Sentence 15 s record; Describe Image 25 s prep / 40 s record; Retell Lecture 10 s prep / 40 s record; Answer Short Question 10 s record; Respond to a Situation 10 s prep / 40 s record; Summarize Written Text **10 minutes**; Write Essay **20 minutes**.
- **Reading is one pooled section budget with no per-item timer.** [Test format: Reading](https://www.pearsonpte.com/pte-academic/test-format/reading/) publishes **"23–30 minutes"** and "5 question types" for Part 2, with no timing on any individual item type. Pearson's own article [Part 2: Reading test introduction](https://www.pearsonpte.com/articles/part-2-reading-test-introduction/) calls it a **"single-timed section"** containing 15–20 questions (that article states 32–41 minutes — an older format figure that conflicts with the current test-format page; treat the section-level *model* as published and the *number* as version-dependent).
- **Reorder Paragraphs is the direct structural analogue of Build a Sentence and is NOT individually timed.** Same Reading page: *"Several text boxes appear on the screen in a random order. Put the text boxes in the correct order."* It lives inside the pooled Reading budget with no clock of its own.
- **Back-navigation is forbidden test-wide.** [PTE Academic Test Taker Handbook](https://www.pearsonpte.com/content/dam/ELL/pte/pearsonpte/pdfs/test-taker-handbook-pte-academic.pdf) p. 12: *"You cannot go back to previous questions to amend your answers."* PTE is therefore the published existence proof that **a pooled budget does not oblige you to allow back-navigation**.
- **Expiry on a per-item-timed task is force-advance.** Same handbook, p. 12: *"For these tasks you have an allotted amount of time to respond. If you do not answer the question within this time, you will be required to move to the next question."*
- **Countdown display: no primary source found** for warning thresholds or a hide affordance.

### Duolingo English Test

- **DET runs both models side by side, and the split is by task shape, not by skill.** The [DET Handbook](https://englishtest-static.duolingo.com/media/resources/GPN%20The%20DET%20Handbook.pdf) publishes a Timing box per question type.
- **Strictly per-item, timer restarting each item:** Read and Select — **"5 seconds"**, one set of 15–18 words, *"The timer for each word begins as soon as the word appears on the screen"* (p. 9–10). Fill in the Blanks — **"20 seconds"**, one set of 6–9 sentences, *"You will have 20 seconds to correctly complete each word"* (p. 15–16). Read Aloud — 20 s per statement.
- **Pooled across a multi-part task, with an official pacing hint:** Interactive Reading — **"7-8 minutes for six tasks"**, and the handbook’s own guidance is *"Since you have a total of 7-8 minutes, allow yourself roughly one minute per task."* (p. 31). Interactive Listening — **"4 minutes"** for a set of 5–6 Listen and Respond questions, then a separate **75 seconds** for Summarize the Conversation (p. 42–44).
- **Explicitly non-pooled across steps of one task:** Interactive Writing is *"Step 1: 5 minutes / Step 2: 3 minutes"* and the handbook says twice: *"There is no advantage to finishing this step before the end of the time limit."* (p. 68–69). DET is thus on record that saved time does **not** carry forward when it did not intend it to.
- **Within-item back-navigation exists where the item is a set of blanks:** Interactive Reading's Complete the Sentences — *"You can fill in the words in any order. First fill in the blanks of the ones you know, then return to the ones you do not."* Forward progress is gated: *"You will not be able to click NEXT until you have selected a response for each blank."*
- **Across items, DET is forward-only in practice:** the handbook's Interactive Listening advice is *"Do not worry if you did not choose the best answers in the previous task"* — a statement only meaningful if you cannot return. **No explicit "you cannot go back" sentence found** in DET's own documentation; marked as inference from primary text, not a published rule.
- **Expiry is auto-advance:** Read Aloud, p. 26 — *"When you are finished recording, click NEXT. If you do not click NEXT, the test will advance automatically when the timer runs out."*
- **Countdown display: always on, with a published anxiety-adjacent instruction to ignore it.** [DET Official Guide 2024](https://englishtest-static.duolingo.com/media/resources/test-taker-guide/DET%20Official%20Guide%202024%20English.pdf) p. 8: *"Try not to look at the timer too often."* Handbook, repeatedly: *"Do not watch the timer, as it may distract you from thinking and speaking clearly."* No hide affordance is published.

### College Board digital SAT / Bluebook

- **The strongest documented comparison case: pooled module budget + full back-navigation + hideable countdown.**
- **Pooled per module.** [Fall 2026 SAT School Day Bluebook Test Directions](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/english-sat-test-directions-bb.pdf) §1.5, verbatim: *"Within each section, the two modules are timed separately. If time permits, you can review your answers in a given module. When the timer reaches zero, you will automatically move on. Once you move on from any module, you cannot return to it."* Module sizes from the same page: Reading and Writing 54 questions in two 27-question modules; Math 44 in two 22-question modules ([How the SAT Is Structured](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/whats-on-the-test/structure): 32 min per R&W module, 35 min per Math module).
- **Back-navigation inside the budget is explicit.** [How does timing work in Bluebook?](https://bluebook.collegeboard.org/help-center/how-does-timing-work-bluebook): *"Each module or part is timed separately." … "You can move back and forth between questions in a module or part until time expires." … "Once you move on from a module or part, you cannot go back to it."*
- **Expiry is auto-advance to the next module, not end-of-test** (the directions quote above). Answers stand as arranged; nothing is scored as skipped by a separate mechanism.
- **Countdown is hideable with a forced reveal, published as a feature.** [Bluebook Testing Tools](https://bluebook.collegeboard.org/students/tools), verbatim: *"A timer will let you know how much time is remaining in each part of the test. **You can hide it until 5 minutes remain, then you'll get an alert.**"* The same page publishes **Mark for Review** and a **Question Menu** *"showing skipped and flagged questions"* — the review affordances that make a pooled budget navigable.
- **No published accessibility/anxiety rationale** for the hide affordance on College Board's own pages; the feature is documented, the reasoning is not.

### IELTS (computer-delivered)

- **Pooled per section, no per-item timing anywhere.** [ielts.org Academic Listening format](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-listening) summary box: Listening "Approximately 30 minutes", 4 parts, 40 questions; Reading "60 minutes (including transfer time)"; Writing "60 minutes"; Speaking "11–14 minutes". Forty Reading questions share one 60-minute budget.
- **Back-navigation across the whole section, with a review flag.** [IDP IELTS (test co-owner), "How computer-delivered IELTS works"](https://ielts.idp.com/canada/prepare/article-how-computer-delivered-ielts-works): Reading — *"can start from anywhere, like the IELTS on paper, change your answer or leave it blank"*; Listening — *"With the navigation button at the bottom of the screen, you can see and check all 40 questions. You can also switch between questions with the forward/backward arrows."*; review flag — *"the question number changes from a square to a circle, so you mark which question you will review."*
- **Two published warning thresholds:** same source — *"there is a stopwatch in the upper middle part of the screen. This chronometer starts to flash in the last 10 minutes and 5 minutes before reading and writing tests end."*
- **Expiry: save and stop.** Same source — *"When the time has run out, your answers are saved and the system automatically shuts down."*
- Caveat: this is a test-owner preparation article rather than a specification; the identical 10/5-minute behaviour is independently confirmed by Cambridge below (same delivery platform family), which is why it is reported as published rather than **[unverified]**.

### Cambridge English (computer-based B2 First and siblings)

- **The best-documented countdown behaviour of any source found.** [Computer-based Cambridge English Qualifications FAQs](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/Images/662077-computer-based-faqs.pdf) (2022), verbatim:
  - Pooled with unrestricted back-navigation: *"If I can't answer a question, can I come back to it again later? — Yes, you can check and change your answers at any time before the end of the test."*
  - Progress affordance: *"The questions you've answered will appear grey and underlined at the bottom of your screen. The unanswered questions will stay as black squares."*
  - Flagging: *"The review button is a useful tool for you to know which questions you want to check again before the end of the exam. Tick review and the question number will become a blue circle."*
  - Always-on countdown, no hide: *"There is a timer at the top of the screen."*
  - **Two warning thresholds, verbatim:** *"For the Reading & Use of English and Writing tests, the clock will turn red and flash when you have ten and five minutes left."*
  - **Expiry, verbatim:** *"The tests will automatically stop when the time finishes."*
  - Listening gets a built-in reserve rather than open review: *"you have two minutes to check your answers before the test ends."*
- Linguaskill (Cambridge's adaptive product) publishes that completion time and item count vary per candidate and that a time limit is one of several stopping criteria ([Adaptive Testing](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/Images/730808-linguaskill-adaptive-testing.pdf)); **no primary source found** for its per-item timing or back-navigation rules — the PDF's body is not machine-readable text.

### GRE and GMAT

- **GRE: pooled per section, unrestricted back-navigation, Mark/Review screen.** [GRE General Test Structure](https://www.ets.org/gre/test-takers/general-test/prepare/test-structure.html) publishes section budgets only — Verbal 12 questions / 18 min and 15 questions / 23 min; Quant 12 / 21 min and 15 / 26 min; Analytical Writing one 30-minute task. The page documents previewing and reviewing questions within a section, Mark and Review features, and changing answers within a section. Adaptivity is **section-level**, which is precisely what buys the freedom to move within a section. **No primary source found** for GRE's expiry behaviour or clock-hiding.
- **GMAT Focus Edition: pooled per section, forward-only during the section, then a bounded review.** [GMAC, "New GMAT exam details"](https://www.gmac.com/resources/learners/how-to-apply/exams-preparation/new-gmat-exam-details): three 45-minute sections (Quant 21 questions, Verbal 23, Data Insights 20). Question Review & Edit lets test takers *"bookmark questions as they move through the test and review them later"*; on finishing a section they see a page listing all questions with bookmarks marked, and may *"review as many questions as they like and edit a maximum of three answers per section"* — using leftover section time. This is the **hybrid**: no free back-navigation while answering, a capped deferred edit at the end.
- GMAC publishes a page framing GMAT Focus design as anxiety-reducing (`mba.com/exams-and-exam-prep/gmat-exam/gmat-focus-helps-reduce-test-anxiety`), but it is JS-rendered and returned no content to two fetch methods — **not citable here**.

### Moodle quiz (open source) — the design space, enumerated in code

- Moodle's quiz module is the only source found that names all three expiry options as first-class settings, which makes it the cleanest vocabulary for issue #50's third open decision.
- **One pooled budget for the whole attempt, never per question.** [`mod/quiz/mod_form.php` L99–L116](https://github.com/moodle/moodle/blob/MOODLE_500_STABLE/mod/quiz/mod_form.php#L99-L116) adds a single `timelimit` duration element; [`lang/en/quiz.php` L1103](https://github.com/moodle/moodle/blob/MOODLE_500_STABLE/mod/quiz/lang/en/quiz.php#L1103): *"If enabled, the time limit is stated on the initial quiz page and a countdown timer is displayed in the quiz navigation block."*
- **Three named expiry semantics** ([`lang/en/quiz.php` L670–L676](https://github.com/moodle/moodle/blob/MOODLE_500_STABLE/mod/quiz/lang/en/quiz.php#L670-L676)): `overduehandlingautosubmit` — *"Open attempts are submitted automatically"* (the default per [MoodleDocs Quiz settings](https://docs.moodle.org/502/en/Quiz_settings)); `overduehandlinggraceperiod` — *"There is a grace period when open attempts can be submitted, but no more questions answered"*; `overduehandlingautoabandon` — *"Attempts must be submitted before time expires, or they are not counted"*.
- **For a learner who is actually present, expiry is always auto-submit-and-stop.** Same file L672: *"If the student is actively working on the quiz at the time, then the countdown timer will always automatically submit the attempt for them"*. The client code confirms it: [`mod/quiz/module.js` L174–L187](https://github.com/moodle/moodle/blob/MOODLE_500_STABLE/mod/quiz/module.js#L174-L187) sets `timeup=1`, forces `finishattempt=0`, and submits the form — the attempt ends where it is; it does not advance through remaining questions.
- **Back-navigation is a two-value setting, not an assumption.** [`lang/en/quiz.php` L592–L595](https://github.com/moodle/moodle/blob/MOODLE_500_STABLE/mod/quiz/lang/en/quiz.php#L592-L595): `navmethod_free` / `navmethod_seq`, help text *"When sequential navigation is enabled a student must progress through the quiz in order and may not return to previous pages nor skip ahead."*
- **Hideable countdown with a forced reveal — independently invented, same shape as Bluebook.** [`mod/quiz/module.js` L189–L200](https://github.com/moodle/moodle/blob/MOODLE_500_STABLE/mod/quiz/module.js#L189-L200): below 100 seconds remaining the timer gains a per-second `timeleft{n}` class **and** `setVisibility(true, false)` runs with `#toggle-timer` disabled, commented *"From now on, the timer should be visible and should not be hideable anymore."* The hidden/shown state is a persisted user preference `quiz_timerhidden` (L141–L143). MoodleDocs states the user-facing rule: *"Students can hide this by pressing the Hide button. The remaining time will reappear when there is less than 100 seconds left."*

### 1EdTech QTI (the interchange standard)

- **QTI formalizes exactly the axes issue #50 is choosing between**, which is useful as vocabulary even though it constrains nothing. [QTI 2.2 Implementation Guide](https://www.imsglobal.org/question/qtiv2p2/imsqti_v2p2_impl.html): `timeLimits` (with `maxTime`/`minTime`) may be attached at `assessmentTest`, `testPart`, `assessmentSection` **or** `assessmentItem` level — pooled and per-item are the same mechanism at different depths. `navigationMode` is `linear` (*"Once the candidate moves on they are not permitted to return"*) or `nonlinear` (*"allowing the candidate to navigate to any item in the test at any time"*). `submissionMode` is `individual` or `simultaneous` (*"The candidate's responses are all submitted together at the end of the testPart"*).
- Expiry default in the standard: *"When the time limit is up the current responses would typically be submitted automatically."*

## Implications for issue #50

**1. Pooled budget size — 360 s. This is a fidelity call, with an estimate inside it.**

- Arithmetic, all inputs published by ETS in the [Test Overview](https://www.ets.org/pdfs/toefl/toefl-ibt-test-overview.pdf): section 23 min = 1380 s; Write an Email 7 min = 420 s (p. 14); Write for an Academic Discussion 10 min = 600 s (p. 15); residual = **1380 − 420 − 600 = 360 s** for the Build a Sentence part.
- **Published fact:** 1380, 420, 600. **Estimate:** that the residual is *entirely* Build a Sentence answering time. It is not — it must also absorb section and part directions, so real answering time is **≤ 360 s**. Using 360 s as the pooled budget is therefore a deliberate upper bound, generous in the direction that helps practice.
- 360 s is **40 s less** than the current implicit total (10 × `ITEM_SECONDS` 40 at `client/src/pages/WritingDrill.tsx:48` and `BS_ITEM_SECONDS` at `client/src/pages/SimulationRun.tsx:813`). Per item it averages 36 s. If tightening banked Sets is unwelcome, 400 s is the "no Set gets harder" alternative — but it is this project's number, not ETS's, and should be labelled as such wherever it is surfaced.
- Do **not** derive the budget from `timeLimitSec` on the Item. Per the issue's own constraint the literal must stay 40 for banked Sets to parse; treat it as advisory metadata and put the budget at the component level.

**2. Back-navigation — allow it, and mark it a FIDELITY call, not a practice-design one.**

- The issue says "ETS's own format is not documented here either way, so this is a practice-design call". **That is refuted for the closest ETS analogue.** ETS's own Essentials delivery client says, for the Build a Sentence part specifically, that a question can be skipped and revisited with Back for as long as the part's time lasts.
- It is corroborated by a consistent cross-product rule: **every product that pools a budget across items allows movement inside it** — Bluebook, IELTS, Cambridge, GRE — and the products that forbid back-navigation (PTE, DET's per-item types, ETS's own Essentials Listening/Speaking) are the ones with **per-item** clocks. PTE Reading is the single counterexample (pooled but forward-only), so "pooled implies back-nav" is a strong convention rather than a law.
- Practical shape to copy: skip + Back + a review/menu affordance that distinguishes answered from unanswered items (Cambridge's grey-underlined vs black squares; Bluebook's Question Menu; GRE's Review screen). Without that affordance, a pooled budget with ten items is navigable only by clicking backwards blindly.

**3. Expiry — submit whatever is arranged for every item and END the task. Practice-design call, but with one dominant published pattern.**

- The three candidates are already named for us by Moodle: auto-submit, grace period, auto-abandon. **Auto-submit is the default in Moodle and the described behaviour in QTI** (*"the current responses would typically be submitted automatically"*), and Moodle's help text is explicit that a learner who is present is *always* auto-submitted.
- The published expiry behaviours split cleanly by budget shape: a **per-item** clock expires by **force-advancing to the next item** (PTE handbook; DET Read Aloud; ETS Essentials essays). A **pooled** clock expires by **stopping the timed unit** — Cambridge *"The tests will automatically stop when the time finishes"*, IELTS *"your answers are saved and the system automatically shuts down"*, Bluebook *"you will automatically move on"* (to the next module, not the next question).
- So the moment issue #50 pools the budget, "submit and advance through the remainder" stops being the analogous behaviour. **Recommendation: on expiry, commit the current arrangement of the item on screen, commit whatever partial arrangements exist for already-visited items, record unvisited items as empty, and move on to Write an Email.** Do not force-advance through ten items in a loop; no comparison product does that, and it produces ten meaningless empty submissions in the same wall-clock instant.
- Nothing in the corpus scores unanswered items as *wrong* by a separate mechanism — they are simply empty and score zero through the normal path. Match that.

**4. Budget display — always-on countdown, low-time escalation, hide toggle optional. Practice-design call, with two independent implementations to copy.**

- **Always-on is the norm** (Cambridge, IELTS, DET, Moodle's default, Bluebook's default) and is what the acceptance criterion "the remaining budget is visible while answering" already asks for.
- **Warning thresholds are published at two levels, twice, by the same platform family:** Cambridge *"the clock will turn red and flash when you have ten and five minutes left"*; IELTS the same 10/5 flash. Those thresholds are sized for 60-minute papers. Scaled to a 360 s budget the analogous marks are roughly **90 s and 45 s** (25% and 12.5%) — that scaling is **our estimate, not published**. `TimerBar` already has an `isLow` threshold at 30 s (`client/src/components/TimerBar.tsx`), which is a reasonable single mark to keep.
- **A hide affordance with a forced reveal is documented twice, independently:** Bluebook *"You can hide it until 5 minutes remain, then you'll get an alert"* (5 min of a 32-min module ≈ 16%); Moodle force-reveals below 100 s and persists the choice as `quiz_timerhidden`. If the hide toggle is built, copy the forced reveal — both implementations have one, and Moodle's code comment gives the reason: the learner must be made aware the unit is about to close.
- **No published accessibility or anxiety rationale was found** on College Board's or Moodle's own documentation pages for the hide affordance. DET is the only test owner that says anything adjacent, and it says the opposite of hiding: *"Try not to look at the timer too often"* / *"Do not watch the timer, as it may distract you"*. Reporting the absence rather than sourcing a rationale from prep blogs.
- **Per-item pace hints have exactly one published precedent, and it is a hint, not a clock:** DET's *"Since you have a total of 7-8 minutes, allow yourself roughly one minute per task."* The equivalent here is a static "≈36 s per item" note next to the pooled countdown — never a second, per-item timer, which would reintroduce the exact coupling the issue is removing.

**5. Drill/Simulation agreement — the acceptance criterion is cheap to satisfy.**

- Both call sites already share `SentenceTiles` and `TimerBar` and both currently hard-code 40 s (`ITEM_SECONDS`, `BS_ITEM_SECONDS`). Replacing both with one pooled budget constant, mounted once for the whole ten-item run rather than keyed per item, makes them agree by construction. The Simulation's existing `deadlineMs` wall-clock mode on `TimerBar` is the correct mode for a 6-minute budget that must survive a laptop-lid close; the Drill can keep the `performance.now()` mode or adopt the same one.
