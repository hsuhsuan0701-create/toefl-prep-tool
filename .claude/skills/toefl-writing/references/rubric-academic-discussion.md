# Write for an Academic Discussion — scoring rubric (0–5)

This file is the TOEFL Prep Tool's own description of the criteria ETS publishes for scoring this
task, written in this project's words. It is not ETS's text. The criteria come from ETS's *Updated
TOEFL iBT® Test Overview* (2026): the task is described on p. 15 and scored by the guide on
pp. 24–25, public at <https://www.ets.org/pdfs/toefl/toefl-ibt-test-overview.pdf>. A user who wants
scoring to read ETS's own wording can install it on their machine; see `docs/official-rubrics.md`.

## The task

- The setting is a class's online discussion board. A professor's post introduces a topic and asks
  the class an opinion question about it; two classmates have already replied, taking different
  sides.
- The test taker adds a post of their own: a position on the professor's question, backed by their
  own reasons, experience or knowledge. Engaging what the classmates said is one way to do that; it
  is not required.
- **10 minutes**, one item, scored 0–5 by AI. The on-screen directions ask the writer to state and
  support an opinion in their own words, and say an effective post runs to **100 words or more**.

## Dimensions

ETS describes what this task measures in four parts. The Judges report one score for each, under
these names:

| Dimension | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **argument & engagement** | Whether the post takes a position on the professor's question and argues for it as a real contribution to this thread — answering, building on or pushing back against what is already there, or drawing on the information the posts provide. |
| **support & cohesion** | The explanations, examples and details that back the position, how relevant and how fully worked out they are, and whether the post holds together. |
| **grammar & vocabulary** | How wide and how accurate the writer's sentence structures and word choices are. |
| **mechanics** | Spelling, punctuation and capitalization. |

ETS's band descriptions speak to relevance, support and language directly. For **mechanics** they
give one explicit allowance, at band 5, and otherwise fold spelling and punctuation into each
band's description of errors; the mechanics lines below say where that is so rather than inventing a
separate ladder.

## How the bands combine

- Every band's description is a profile. Place a response at the band whose profile its dimensions
  fit as a whole. Unlike the email rubric, this one has no band where a single weakness is enough
  on its own.
- The item score is a judgement of the whole post against these profiles, not an average of the
  four dimension scores.
- ETS's directions tie an effective post to 100 words or more, but the bands themselves state no
  length rule. A post that stops short usually lacks the developed support the upper bands describe.

## Score bands

### Score: 5

**In short:** a relevant post that gets its ideas across with great clarity, written with sure
command of English throughout.

- **argument & engagement** — The post is clearly relevant to the professor's question, and its
  position comes across with great clarity.
- **support & cohesion** — The explanations, examples and details are relevant and thoroughly worked
  out; nothing the argument depends on is left hanging.
- **grammar & vocabulary** — A range of sentence structures, handled well, and words that are exact
  and used the way a fluent writer uses them.
- **mechanics** — The only slips are the kind a capable writer makes against the clock: an
  occasional typo, a commonly misspelled word, a homophone swap such as *there*/*their*.

Language errors of any other kind are close to absent.

### Score: 4

**In short:** a relevant post whose ideas a reader takes in without effort, though the writing is
less polished than at 5.

- **argument & engagement** — The post is a relevant reply to the professor's question, and its
  ideas are easy to understand.
- **support & cohesion** — Explanations, examples and details are relevant and developed enough,
  though not as fully as at 5.
- **grammar & vocabulary** — A range of sentence structures and suitable, if less exact, words. A
  small number of grammar or word errors.
- **mechanics** — No separate standard: spelling, punctuation and capitalization count among the
  band's small number of errors.

**What separates 4 from 5:** support that is adequate rather than thorough, structures that vary
without being handled with distinction, words that are suitable rather than exact, and errors that
are few rather than nearly absent.

### Score: 3

**In short:** a post that is mostly relevant and mostly understandable, from a writer with some
command of English.

- **argument & engagement** — The post mostly addresses the question, though parts of it may drift
  from it.
- **support & cohesion** — Some piece of the support falls short: an explanation, example or detail
  is missing, unclear, or does not bear on the point.
- **grammar & vocabulary** — Some variety of sentence structure and a reasonable range of words.
  Errors in how sentences are built, in word forms or in idiom are noticeable to a reader.
- **mechanics** — No separate standard: spelling and punctuation errors count among the band's
  noticeable errors.

**What separates 3 from 4:** at 4 everything is relevant and every point is backed; at 3 something
in the support is missing, unclear or off the point, and the errors are noticeable rather than few.

### Score: 2

**In short:** the writer is trying to take part in the discussion, but weak control of English may
make the ideas hard to follow.

- **argument & engagement** — The ideas are only partly relevant to the question.
- **support & cohesion** — Ideas are poorly developed: claims arrive with little explanation or
  example behind them.
- **grammar & vocabulary** — A narrow range of sentence structures and words, and errors in how
  sentences are built, in word forms or in word use that build up across the post.
- **mechanics** — No separate standard: spelling and punctuation errors count among the band's
  accumulated errors.

**What separates 2 from 3:** at 3 the reader can follow the post and most of it is relevant; at 2
the ideas are hard to follow, only partly relevant, or barely supported.

### Score: 1

**In short:** an attempt to join the discussion that does not succeed; weak control of English may
keep the writer from expressing ideas at all.

- **argument & engagement** — Words and phrases show the writer is trying to respond to the task,
  but they add up to few coherent ideas, or none.
- **support & cohesion** — There is little or no support, because there are few ideas to support.
- **grammar & vocabulary** — A severely narrow range of structures and words, and serious errors
  throughout.
- **mechanics** — No separate standard: spelling and punctuation errors count among the band's
  serious and frequent errors.

Little of the language is the writer's own: whatever reads coherently is mostly lifted from the
professor's or classmates' posts.

**What separates 1 from 2:** at 2 there are ideas, however hard to follow; at 1 there are few
coherent ideas, or the coherent parts are borrowed.

### Score: 0

Any of these: nothing was written; the writer refuses to engage with the topic; the response is not
in English; it consists entirely of text copied from the prompt; it has no connection to the
prompt; or it is random keystrokes.

## Notes on this description

- ETS introduces every band of this guide the same way, so every band here reads as a profile. The
  Write an Email rubric reads its two lowest bands differently; do not carry that reading over.
- The dimension lines are this project's arrangement of ETS's band descriptions under the four
  measured skills, so that each Judge dimension can be scored against its own line.

## Note on Build a Sentence

This skill's AGGREGATE mode never scores **Build a Sentence**, the remaining Writing task: ETS
publishes no 0–5 guide for it. Its overview (p. 23) introduces the Writing scoring guides as
covering only the email and the academic discussion, and its specifications document (p. 5) counts
all ten Build a Sentence items as scored by machine, none by AI. Here a response earns the item only
when its tiles match one of the orders the Set accepts, so `generation-guide.md` has every Set list
all of its accepted orders.
