# Take an Interview — scoring rubric (0–5)

This file is the TOEFL Prep Tool's own description of the criteria ETS publishes for scoring this
task, written in this project's words. It is not ETS's text. The criteria come from ETS's *Updated
TOEFL iBT® Test Overview* (2026): the task is described on p. 19 and scored by the guide on
pp. 27–28, public at <https://www.ets.org/pdfs/toefl/toefl-ibt-test-overview.pdf>. A user who wants
scoring to read ETS's own wording can install it on their machine; see `docs/official-rubrics.md`.

## The task

- A recorded interviewer holds a short conversation with the test taker, framed by a situation
  such as a scholarship application or a research study.
- **Four questions** on the interview's topic, about the test taker's own experience and views. The
  first ones ask for facts and personal experience; the later ones ask for an opinion on a wider
  issue, with reasons.
- **45 seconds** to answer each. All four answers are scored 0–5 by AI.

> **Not an ETS fact:** ETS's overview states no preparation time for these questions. This app
> plays each question and opens the response window with no separate preparation period. Tell a
> learner that this is how the app models the task, not that ETS gives no preparation time.

## Dimensions

ETS measures four things here. The Judges report one score for each, under these names:

| Dimension | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **topical elaboration** | Whether the answer responds to the question that was asked and develops it with relevant, connected detail, reasons or examples. |
| **pace & pausing** | Whether the speaker keeps up the rate of ordinary conversation, and whether pauses fall where a natural speaker would put them or break up the flow. |
| **intelligibility & prosody** | Whether the words are easy to understand, and whether stress, rhythm and intonation help carry the meaning. |
| **grammar & vocabulary** | The range and accuracy of the speaker's grammar and words, and how precisely they let the speaker say what they mean. |

Pauses and filler words are part of natural speech. They count against **pace & pausing** only when
they stop the answer from flowing; a listener's experience, not the number of pauses, is the test.

## Score bands

### Score: 5

**In short:** a complete answer to the question, delivered clearly and fluently.

- **topical elaboration** — The answer stays on the question and is developed well.
- **pace & pausing** — A comfortable conversational pace throughout; pauses come where a natural
  speaker would make them.
- **intelligibility & prosody** — Easy to understand, and the speaker's rhythm and intonation help
  carry the meaning.
- **grammar & vocabulary** — A range of grammar and words, used accurately enough to say precisely
  what the speaker means.

### Score: 4

**In short:** the answer responds to the question and is reasonably clear.

- **topical elaboration** — On the question and developed, though the links between sentences may
  be weak or missing, so ideas sit side by side rather than leading into one another.
- **pace & pausing** — The pace is mostly good; some pauses may interrupt the flow slightly.
- **intelligibility & prosody** — Pronunciation, rhythm and intonation do not get in the way of the
  meaning, though a listener may now and then need a moment to catch a word or phrase.
- **grammar & vocabulary** — Good enough to get general meanings across most of the time.

**What separates 4 from 5:** ideas that are developed but not tied together, pauses that slightly
interrupt the flow, the occasional word that takes effort to catch, and language that conveys the
general sense rather than the exact one.

### Score: 3

**In short:** the answer responds to the question, but it is thinly developed, not fully clear, or
both.

- **topical elaboration** — Generally on the question, but the development may be fairly thin.
- **pace & pausing** — Pauses are frequent or long enough that the delivery becomes choppy, and
  filler words are frequent. (This is a separate criterion from elaboration; weigh the two
  separately.)
- **intelligibility & prosody** — At times the listener has trouble understanding, because words are
  mispronounced or stress and rhythm are off.
- **grammar & vocabulary** — Narrow or inaccurate grammar and vocabulary noticeably limit how
  precisely and clearly the speaker can express ideas.

**What separates 3 from 4:** at 4 the pauses barely touch the flow and the listener rarely has to
work; at 3 the delivery is choppy, understanding sometimes takes effort, and the language holds the
meaning back.

### Score: 2

**In short:** the speaker tries to answer, but what they say is not developed into anything
meaningful, or cannot be understood well enough to be.

- **topical elaboration** — The answer connects to the question only slightly. It offers little or
  no relevant development, or consists mostly of words taken from the question itself.
- **intelligibility & prosody** — Limited: much of the time it is hard to tell what the speaker
  intends.
- **grammar & vocabulary** — A very narrow range of grammar and words.
- **pace & pausing** — ETS describes no separate pace standard at this band. Rate it no higher than
  the band's overall picture of an answer that barely gets across.

**What separates 2 from 3:** at 3 the answer is on the question and mostly understandable; at 2 it
barely addresses the question, recycles the question's words, or its meaning is often hard to
discern.

### Score: 1

**In short:** the answer barely touches the question and may show very little control of the
language.

- **topical elaboration** — Only loosely related to the words of the interviewer's question.
- **intelligibility & prosody** — Most of the answer cannot be understood.
- **grammar & vocabulary** — The answer consists mostly of single words or short phrases standing
  on their own.
- **pace & pausing** — ETS describes no separate pace standard at this band. Rate it no higher than
  the band's overall picture.

**What separates 1 from 2:** at 2 there is connected speech that touches the question; at 1 there
are mostly isolated words or phrases, and most of it cannot be understood.

### Score: 0

Any of these: no response; nothing in it can be understood; it contains no English; it has nothing
to do with the question; or it consists only of remarks such as "I don't know".

## Notes on this description

- The same five conditions decide a 0 for Listen and Repeat.
- At band 3, ETS's published layout runs the elaboration criterion and the pause criterion into one
  line. They are two criteria, listed under two dimensions here.
